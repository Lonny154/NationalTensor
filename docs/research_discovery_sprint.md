# National Tensor — research discovery sprint

## Decision for review

**Recommended target:** explain the saved two-slab pipeline-stage inversion on the RTX 4060 Ti. In one 512×3072×768 FP16 screening run, reducing `num_stages` from three to two raised profiled active-warps occupancy but made the two-stage variant slower in 17 of 20 paired rounds. The observed paired median ratio was `stage-3 / stage-2 = 0.951883`; independent timing confirmation is missing. The difference is close to the run's largest A/A median absolute ratio deviation (`4.81%` effect versus `4.15%` A/A), so reproducing it is the first scientific task. Neither occupancy nor pipeline overlap has been established as the cause.

This report uses saved measurements and compiler/profiler records. It proposes experiments for human review; it does not authorize implementation, GPU execution, or a Muse request. No discovery is claimed yet.

### 1. Pipeline-stage inversion

**Measured:** The stage-reduction run changed only `num_stages` (`3 → 2`) in the reviewed two-slab GEMM at FP16 `512×3072×768`, with `64×128×32` tiles and four warps. All 12 scoped correctness checks passed. The 20-round eager CUDA-event screening gave median latencies `0.061270` versus `0.064336 ms`, with stage 3 faster in 17 rounds; the median paired ratio was `0.951883`. A/A median absolute deviations were `4.149%` for stage 3 and `1.226%` for stage 2. One Nsight capture showed stage 2 with higher active-warps occupancy (`27.62%` versus `15.85%`).

**Why unresolved:** A common occupancy-only explanation predicts stage 2 might be faster. The measured direction is opposite, but the timing effect barely exceeds one A/A noise summary and lacks independent confirmation. Compiler code also changes with `num_stages`; occupancy is not an isolated treatment.

**Competing explanations and falsifiers:**

| Hypothesis | Minimum distinguishing observation | Would weaken or falsify it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1: three stages hide global-memory latency or pipeline waits better despite lower occupancy. | Confirm the stage-3 latency edge in fresh processes; compare relevant wait/scoreboard counters and asynchronous-copy/barrier sequences in SASS under matched shapes. Test a longer K with the same M/N grid to see whether the edge grows with pipeline depth. | The edge disappears on confirmation; or stage 2 has no greater wait pressure and the edge does not depend on K depth, within measurement sensitivity. |
| H2: a separate compiler/code-generation difference dominates, such as registers, instruction scheduling, spills, or tensor-pipe utilization. | Compare compiled loop-body SASS, resources, spills, executed instructions, and tensor activity for stage 2 versus 3; identify a concrete difference that predicts the direction. | Executable differences reduce to expected stage-buffering mechanics, or the proposed codegen marker does not track latency across repeated captures/variants. |
| H3: the apparent gap is timing-state or host-submission noise. | Repeat balanced A/A and stage-2/stage-3 pairs in fresh processes with reversed order; retain every raw block, clock state, and host submission measure. | The stage-3 edge recurs beyond each session's A/A spread under independent confirmation and a matched graph-mode diagnostic, while order and idle state fail to explain it. |

**Noise and feasibility:** The recorded eager blocks include 100 Python launches, and other local diagnostics show rare first-block excursions far larger than the nominal kernel time. Nsight replay perturbs caches/clocks. The RTX 4060 Ti already compiled and ran both variants and returned occupancy counters, so the core test is accessible; some detailed stall or asynchronous-copy metrics may be unavailable. Estimated effort: roughly one focused implementation/review session plus two independent measurement sessions, conditional on hardware access.

### 2. Idle first-block latency

**Measured:** The saved 10-second-idle factorial produced first-block `810.8` and `850.7 µs/launch` without a preparation block, versus `67.4` and `71.5 µs/launch` for immediate captures. With preparation, the first preparation blocks were `780.8` and `987.6 µs`; one first timed block still reached `418.7 µs`. Both immediate settled captures stayed below `66 µs`.

**Why unresolved:** Ten excluded warmups per side and a 100-launch preparation block did not reliably absorb the effect. It may represent a GPU wake/clock transition, driver/host submission delay inside a CUDA-event block, cache or residency state, or an interaction among them. Two repetitions per cell and uncontrolled clocks prevent causal attribution.

**Minimum discriminator:** In several fresh processes, randomize immediate versus 10-second idle and balanced preparation/no-preparation cells. Record GPU clock/P-state telemetry before and during each block if available, host submission and event durations, and the first few 100-launch blocks without deleting outliers. A CUDA Graph version with the *same kernel and fixed addresses* can test host-submission sensitivity, but its absolute times have different boundaries. A wake/clock hypothesis weakens if excursions recur at stable clocks; a host-submission hypothesis weakens if graph replay preserves device-event excursions; a cold-cache hypothesis weakens if controlled cache priming leaves them unchanged. The RTX 4060 Ti and existing diagnostic can run it. Complexity is low to medium, but clock telemetry may be too coarse for a short transient.

### 3. Split-K shape boundary in prior work

**Measured:** In Matmul Inspector's fixed-address graph study on the same RTX 4060 Ti, complete Split-2 was faster than conventional GEMM in two M=32 sessions, with 8/8 favorable pairs in each. At M=128 the differences crossed zero and remained within identical-kernel variation. The complete Split-2 operation includes partial GEMM and reduction; the configured conventional grids have 16 and 32 output CTAs, respectively. M=256 and M=512 were stopped before timing by the unchanged correctness gate.

**Why unresolved:** The pattern could reflect too few conventional CTAs at M=32, a K-splitting critical-path benefit, changing scratch/reduction cost, or fixed-address graph cache behavior. CTA count is configured parallelism, not measured occupancy. At M=128, identical-kernel A/A maximum gaps of `6.38` and `4.86 µs` covered the observed A/B differences; even the M=32 sessions had `3.87` and `4.01 µs` A/A maxima. Graph replay, address reuse, and shape-dependent scratch size are confounders. The result is shape-specific, not a proven crossover or a new National Tensor speedup.

**Minimum discriminator:** Preserve the prior graph and correctness protocol, instrument partial-GEMM and reduction phases separately, measure achieved occupancy/waves and cache traffic if supported, and add a correctly validated intermediate M before seeking a crossover. A CTA-parallelism explanation weakens if occupancy/wave data do not change in the predicted direction; a reduction-overhead explanation weakens if the reduction remains a small fraction as the advantage vanishes; a fixed-address-cache explanation weakens if controlled address/cache perturbations preserve the M=32 advantage. The GPU is suitable, but adapting external artifacts and resolving larger-shape numerical mismatches make this the least feasible deadline target.

## Why Experiment 005 was not selected

Hilbert scheduling has extensive validated correctness and saved compiler outputs, but the eight screening comparisons show no confirmed latency advantage. The first map-setup cost is a real, large **setup** outlier, not kernel timing; first-use CUDA initialization, allocation/transfer, and host synchronization are unresolved competing causes. The later one-launch locality probe also contains an impossible derived hit percentage, so it cannot be used as a causal cache result. A shape-order-randomized, split CPU-build/allocate/transfer timer could investigate setup, but it would answer a startup-cost question rather than the selected steady-state GPU scheduling question. The prior Matmul Inspector results above provide stronger measured shape-dependent performance context without transferring their claims to Hilbert.

## Recommended experiment — confirm and explain the stage-depth inversion

**Frozen question:** On this RTX 4060 Ti and reviewed two-slab GEMM, does the three-stage specialization repeatedly run faster than the two-stage specialization at FC1 `512×3072×768`, despite its lower measured occupancy ceiling? If so, is extra pipeline overlap more consistent with the evidence than a codegen difference or timing-state artifact?

1. **Preflight and baseline.** Human reviewer checks the reviewed two-slab source hash, original [stage-reduction record](../evaluations/stage-reduction/20261028T140056186673Z/result.json), benchmark method, and proposed new run namespace before GPU work. Baseline is `num_stages=3`; primary variant is `num_stages=2`. Hold kernel body, tile sizes `64×128×32`, four warps, dtype, strides, input distribution, output allocation, and device fixed. Compile both afresh; save source and environment hashes. No generated Muse kernel is executed.
2. **Correctness gate.** Run both variants against the established FP16 `torch.mm` reference with reduced-precision reduction disabled during reference generation, using the prior tolerances `rtol=atol=0.02`. Include seeds 42/43 and the previously checked `512×3072×768`, `509×3071×769`, and `65×129×33` shapes. Compare the variants directly as well. Any failure stops timing for that variant; record the failure without changing tolerance.
3. **Primary paired timing.** Use the existing preallocated-output, warmup, balanced-order CUDA-event block method on `512×3072×768`. In each of **two fresh processes**, run stage-3 A/A and stage-2 A/A calibration, then at least 20 stage-3/stage-2 paired rounds of 100 launches per timed block. Reverse overall variant order in the second process and save every block, order, host submission duration, CUDA-event duration, clock/temperature readings if available, compilation/warmup state, and an idle-gap marker. No sample is discarded. Keep the ratio orientation `stage-3 time / stage-2 time` throughout.
4. **Independent confirmation.** On a different session with fresh inputs and reversed order seed, repeat correctness and at least 14 balanced paired rounds at 200 launches per block, plus A/A checks. For every session, retain paired ratios and compute a seeded 2,000-resample percentile interval for the paired median, using the same recorded rule for all sessions. Treat the same-direction medians in both fresh-process screening sessions and confirmation, intervals that exclude one, and an effect larger than session-specific A/A variation as **support for a repeatable latency difference**. Treat mixed directions, interval overlap, or an effect comparable to A/A as null/inconclusive. These descriptive gates are not a significance test or a universal noise bound.
5. **Mechanism probes only if timing warrants them.** Compare TTIR, TTGIR, LLIR, PTX, cubin/SASS, static and allocated registers, shared memory, spills, asynchronous-copy/wait/barrier sequences, and the affected loop body. Capture at least two bounded Nsight Compute runs per variant in counterbalanced A–B–B–A order, separately from timing. Prior local captures demonstrate availability of `launch__registers_per_thread`, `launch__shared_mem_per_block`, `sm__warps_active.avg.pct_of_peak_sustained_active`, `smsp__inst_executed_pipe_tensor.avg.pct_of_peak_sustained_active`, `smsp__warps_issue_stalled_long_scoreboard.avg.per_cycle_active`, `smsp__warps_issue_stalled_barrier.avg.per_cycle_active`, `dram__bytes.sum.per_second`, `lts__t_bytes.sum.per_second`, and `profiler__replayer_passes`. Check metric availability and units first; unavailable counters stay null. Never substitute profiler replay duration for the paired event result.
6. **One controlled scaling check.** If the primary result repeats and the new shape passes correctness, compare the same stage pair at `512×3072×1536`: M/N grid and tile configuration stay fixed while K depth doubles. Save a fresh compiler comparison because K changes specialization. A secondary `2048×3072×768` grid can test occupancy sensitivity only after another correctness gate; its different working set prevents a one-factor causal claim. These are bounded follow-ups, not a search for a favorable shape.

**Interpretation rules:** A repeatable stage-3 latency edge with higher stage-2 occupancy, plus higher wait pressure for stage 2 and a K-depth trend, would favor H1; it would not prove a unique cause. A distinct codegen/resource marker that tracks the edge would favor H2. A disappearing edge or dependence on idle/order/host measures would favor H3. If timing confirms but counters disagree or are unavailable, report the latency phenomenon with its mechanism unresolved. No positive latency result authorizes deployment or generalization to other GPUs.

## Muse's role after human-approved measurements

To test **discovery**, give Muse a bounded, hash-bound observation package without labeling the selected anomaly or showing the human H1–H3 explanations above. Include the stage-2/stage-3 raw paired and A/A samples, correctness status, timing boundaries, compiler differences, valid profiler counters with units and replay caveats, and a few comparably structured controls from Experiment 005 and the idle diagnostic. Preserve source/run identities and mark every missing stage or counter. Ask Muse to select the most informative *measured* unexpected pattern, cite exact observations, distinguish it from a mere optimization idea, give at least two competing hardware or measurement explanations with falsifiers, and propose the smallest test that separates them. A human then checks whether Muse identified the stage inversion or a better-supported anomaly, whether its causal predictions are specific, and whether its test would actually discriminate them. Do not score it for repeating an anomaly named in the prompt.

Only after that independent identification should an operator compare Muse's explanations with the human preregistered alternatives and show any follow-up data. Keep Muse's prose advisory, validate factual citations to the new package, and require human review before implementation or GPU execution. A model answer is not itself a measurement. No Muse request is authorized by this protocol.
