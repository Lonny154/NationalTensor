## Blind-scoring integrity and full attempt accounting

The implementation shuffles A and B **within each proposal** using the frozen seed, removes condition labels and proposal hashes from reviewer cards, and writes the assignment separately. The operator should deliver only the blind packet and rubric, avoid showing filenames or discussion that identify conditions, and restrict access to the assignment and request audits until the twelve scored entries are recorded and hash-bound. Preserve the original rubric text and scoring criteria; do not tune them after seeing decisions, scores, or the reveal. Score reasoning quality rather than favoring `proceed`, `revise`, or `deprioritize`.

Blindness is imperfect because B may mention “independent criticism” or mirror its wording. The reviewer should record the suspected cue in each card's `possible_unblinding` field, even if uncertain, and explain any influence on the score. A neutral operator may check the packet for accidental assignment metadata before delivery, without rewriting decision text or changing the frozen review procedure. Report the number and pattern of suspected unblindings with the results.

The reveal's condition means and six paired B-minus-A differences are **descriptive subjective scores** from a small pilot. They are not a significance test, measured compute savings, or proof that criticism improves research decisions. Keep the paired scores, justifications, recommendation categories, and any discordant cases visible. Do not change the rubric or select only favorable pairs after inspecting the reveal.

Maintain an append-only attempt ledger alongside any eventual report: run ID, proposal, stage/condition, case ID where relevant, request timestamp, prompt/version and hashes, source hash, response ID/model, status and rejection reason, audit/manifest paths and hashes, revalidation ID/status, binding provenance, and whether an attempt was included in the final twelve.
